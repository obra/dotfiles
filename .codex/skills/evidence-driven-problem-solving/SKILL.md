# Evidence-driven problem solving

Adapted from Boris Cherny's ["I am often wrong"](https://borischerny.com/management,/product/2026/09/19/I-am-often-wrong.html) (2026-09-19).

1. Inventory relevant facts, assumptions, constraints, and unknowns.
2. Investigate unknowns that could change the decision; prefer a targeted check.
3. State who faces what problem and why it matters, separately from proposed solutions.
4. Choose the simplest supported approach. Flag complexity caused by an unclear problem.
5. Translate the user's goal into observable success criteria and a verification method.
6. Execute a bounded, authorized increment promptly; compare results with those criteria.

When evidence contradicts an assumption, explain what changed and revise the problem,
approach, or success criteria as needed. Preserve the user's objective; changing it
requires agreement. Avoid restarting without a material reason.

Invite focused feedback when framing remains uncertain; offer specific corrections
promptly. Apply feedback to this method too.

Report the problem, approach, success criteria, and next action concisely; identify
revisions and their evidence. Stop when success is verified.
